Research Findings

Flat Social Attention pose capacity at 5 FPS

Immutable snapshot 2026-09-17

Overview

Research question

How much mouse trajectory and pose structure can the flat Social Attention architecture preserve under controlled 5 FPS prediction?

Conclusion

Flat Social Attention learns useful one-step mouse geometry. Complete pose degrades during long sampled autoregressive rollout.

The matched single-mouse model improves body scale and mouse-local keypoint position. Reliable improvements were absent for orientation, heading, and relative ordering.

Every learned variant performs worse than persistence on its held-out benchmark.

Plain-language conclusion

The model predicts a reasonable next pose when it receives correct previous poses. Errors accumulate when it continues from its own predictions.

Using one mouse reduces body distortion. Repeating the final observed pose still gives more accurate future predictions.

Facts to carry forward

  • Single-mouse training gives the strongest learned body-scale result.
  • Lower body-scale error does not imply correct orientation or keypoint order.
  • The main measured failure occurs during autoregressive rollout.
  • Predicted Gaussian uncertainty has poor coverage.
  • Results cover one training seed and one rollout seed.

Findings

Body scale improves without cross-mouse edges

Both variants without cross-mouse edges have lower BLE than the dense graph. Single mouse also improves body-frame keypoint displacement.

Plain language: The dense model stretches and collapses the mouse more often. Reducing interaction with other mice helps preserve body-part distances.

Scope: one training seed. The BLE interval excludes zero for both comparisons.

Body scale does not establish complete pose quality

SOE, BHE, and ROE show no reliable improvement for either no-cross-mouse-edge variant.

Plain language: Body parts can remain the right distance apart while the mouse faces the wrong way or uses the wrong keypoint order.

Scope: each paired confidence interval includes zero.

Actor count affects the result

Within-mouse triplet and single mouse both remove cross-mouse edges. Single mouse has lower paired trajectory, velocity, BLE, and body-frame displacement errors.

Plain language: Managing three mice remains difficult even after removing their direct graph connections. One mouse gives the model a smaller recurrent problem.

Interpretation: the single-mouse gain cannot be assigned to cross-edge removal alone.

Autoregressive rollout causes the largest degradation

Teacher-forced Gaussian-mean P50 errors remain near 4 px BLE, 9 degrees SOE, and 0.06 ROE. Errors rise sharply during sampled rollout.

Plain language: Correct previous poses keep the model stable. Model-generated poses carry errors into the next step.

Interpretation: the architecture has useful one-step capacity. Long rollout stability remains weak.

Uncertainty coverage is poor

Final nominal 95% coverage is 5.2% for dense, 4.4% for within-mouse triplet, and 12.9% for single mouse.

Plain language: The model reports narrow uncertainty regions that rarely contain the real future keypoint.

Single mouse has the best coverage. Its 12.9% result remains far below 95%.

Unsupported and inconclusive hypotheses

  • Cross-edge removal improves ROE: inconclusive. Both ROE intervals include zero.
  • Lower validation NLL predicts a better rollout: rejected for these checkpoints. Within-mouse triplet has the lowest NLL and the worst sampled trajectory metrics.
  • Cross-mouse edges cause all pose failure: unsupported. BLE changes while SOE, BHE, and ROE remain poor.
  • The flat model has no pose capacity: unsupported. Teacher-forced results show useful one-step capacity.

Next experiments

Run each intervention independently against the matched single-mouse control.

Auxiliary angle loss

Hypothesis: Penalizing anatomical edge-angle error may improve SOE and BHE, with a possible ROE benefit.

Plain language: Add a penalty when connected body parts point in the wrong directions.

Control: Keep data, graph, optimizer budget, Gaussian NLL, and seed fixed.

Success: Paired SOE or BHE improvement with no material cADE, cFDE, BLE, CVE, or KVE regression.

Scheduled sampling

Hypothesis: Training with an increasing share of model-generated recurrent inputs may reduce exposure bias.

Plain language: Let the model practice continuing from its own mistakes during training.

Control: Keep architecture, losses, data, optimizer budget, and seed fixed.

Success: A smaller teacher-forced-to-autoregressive gap and paired sampled-rollout improvements.

Conditional combined experiment

Combine angle loss and scheduled sampling after at least one isolated ablation improves its target metrics.

Plain language: Measure each idea alone before testing them together.

Success: Preserve the isolated gains without adding regressions in the other metric group.

Optional centroid-pose factorization

Hypothesis: Separate arena-space centroid motion from mouse-local keypoint offsets, then reconstruct global keypoints from centroid, heading, and local pose.

Plain language: Predict where the mouse moves separately from how its body is arranged.

Reason for deferral: This changes the representation and architecture more than the first two ablations.

Glossary

Social Attention

A Structural RNN that models node motion and graph-edge interactions. This report evaluates its flat keypoint version.

Back to first use

Autoregressive rollout

A prediction process where each generated frame becomes input to the next step. Errors can accumulate across the future horizon.

Back to first use

Persistence

A non-trained baseline that repeats the final observed pose for every future frame.

Back to first use

Cross-mouse edges

Graph connections between keypoints that belong to different mice.

Back to first use

cADE

Mean centroid displacement error over future frames. It measures whether the mouse occupies the correct area over time.

Back to first use

cFDE

Centroid displacement error at the final predicted frame. It measures the final whole-mouse location error.

Back to first use

CVE

Centroid velocity error. It measures whole-mouse speed and direction error between frames.

Back to first use

KVE

Keypoint velocity error. It measures motion error for individual body points.

Back to first use

BLE

Bone-length error across anatomical edges. It measures body stretching and collapse.

Back to first use

SOE

Skeleton orientation error across connected keypoints. It measures incorrect segment direction.

Back to first use

BHE

Body-heading error. It measures the difference between predicted and actual whole-mouse facing direction.

Back to first use

ROE

Relative-ordering error. It measures keypoint order disagreements in the actual mouse coordinate frame.

Back to first use

BFK-ADE and BFK-FDE

Body-frame keypoint displacement errors after accounting for whole-mouse position and orientation. They measure mouse-local point placement.

Back to first use

Confidence interval

A range produced by resampling source windows. An interval that crosses zero cannot identify which compared model has lower error in this evaluation.

Back to first use

Teacher forcing

Evaluation or training where the correct previous frame is supplied before each next-step prediction.

Back to first use

Calibration and coverage

Calibration compares predicted uncertainty with observed error. A nominal 95% region should contain about 95% of actual targets.

Back to first use

NLL

Negative log-likelihood, the Gaussian training and validation objective. Lower NLL rewards probability assigned to the next true keypoint.

Back to first use

Exposure bias

The mismatch between training on correct previous frames and inference on model-generated previous frames.
